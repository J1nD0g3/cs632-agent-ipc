# Review: "Messages or Files? Revisiting IPC for LLM Agent Pipelines" (methods and statistics)

## Summary

The paper compares five ways of passing results between three LLM agents (Researcher, Writer, Reviewer) on bundled HotpotQA/2Wiki questions. The channels are full-history copy (A1), the same copy reordered for prefix caching (A1-zc), pipe (A2), shared files (B), and files with an index (C). All agents are Qwen3 models served by vLLM. The main run (Qwen3-8B, RTX 3090, 35k-token KV cache, 150 tasks per dataset and k) reports tokens sent, prefill, and accuracy. Later runs on RTX PRO 6000 add a serving-time study, a 2x2 "crossed" run per model size (reply vs tool-call writing x copied vs file delivery: A1r, A1w, Bs, B), cache-size and model-size sweeps, a k=8 test of one vs three Researchers with per-Researcher folders (Bf), and k=16 repairs (write guard Bn, heading rule Bh). The headline findings are:

1. Broadcast is the most accurate channel.
2. Zero-copy computes about 40% of broadcast's prefill with a paired accuracy interval of -2.9 to 1.4 points, but saves no serving time.
3. Tool-call writing of notes costs accuracy when the notes are copied forward.
4. Parallel writers sharing one folder cause most of B's fan-out loss, and separate folders recover it.

The experimental hygiene is well above the workshop norm. All arms in a comparison run on the same tasks, job order is shuffled, KV is salted per task and channel, intervals are paired task-level bootstraps, Holm correction is applied to the 2x2, a negative control (A1) is used in the fan-out test, and the harness rescue is disclosed. However, by re-deriving numbers from `results_raw/` I found one measurement bug that reverses an explanatory claim. The "reply stored as a file" arm (Bs) also does not isolate what the paper says it isolates. And several runs that bear on the claims were left out of the paper.

## Score: 3 / 5 (borderline; accept if the Bs/open-rate issue is fixed and the claims built on it are rescoped)

## Confidence: 4 / 5

I read `pipeline.py`, `workspace.py`, `metrics.py`, `alignfacts.py`, `analyze.py`, `stats.py`, the relevant parts of `run.py` and `paper_numbers.py`, the configs and env files of the cited runs, and recomputed the key quantities from `results_raw/*/records.jsonl.gz` and `rows.jsonl.gz`.

## Strengths

- **Controlled, paired design.** Every arm in a comparison runs the same tasks in one run, with shuffled job order (`run.py` shuffles `(task, channel)` jobs) and per-task `cache_salt`. Paired task-level bootstrap intervals respect the clustering of questions within a task.
- **The 2x2 crossing (Table 1) is the right design** for splitting a loss whose stepwise split depends on order. Running all four arms in one run per model size removes cross-run drift from the simple effects.
- **A Holm-corrected sign-flip permutation test over the 16 simple effects**, with the corrected and uncorrected counts both stated in the abstract. That is unusually candid.
- **The k=8 fan-out test uses broadcast as a negative control** (-0.9, CI -3.9 to 2.0) next to B's -21.2, and the difference-in-differences is computed. This makes the fan-out claim the strongest causal result in the paper.
- **Several honest caveats.** The rescue rate is disclosed with a strict-scoring bound. AlignScore's weak agreement (kappa 0.29) is disclosed. Cross-run comparisons are labeled. The separate-folders arm's path confound is stated.
- **Replications exist and agree.** The k=16 B gap is 33.9 on the 3090 (t08_large), 33.9 on the PRO 6000 (p07_k16_kv100k), and 30.4 in p12. The write effect in p10 (-8.7, -5.6, -0.2, -4.4) closely matches p16 (-8.7, -4.7, 0.2, -4.0) on the same tasks.
- **Every number in the text is generated by script** (`paper_numbers.py` writes `numbers.tex`), so it can be checked.

## Weaknesses (most important first)

### 1. The Writer-open metric is wrong at 14B, and the Bs arm confounds "how notes are written" with whether they can be found. (text/analysis only, plus needs new runs)

The open rate in `round10()` counts a Writer read as a notes read only if the path does not start with `docs`. At 14B, the B Researcher saves its notes as `docs/Q1_facts.txt`, `docs/Q2_facts.txt`, and `docs/Q3_facts.txt` in 119 of 150 tasks, so those reads are counted as document reads.

Recomputed by matching the Writer's reads against the files the Researcher wrote, B's Writer at 14B opens the notes in **99%** of tasks, not 17%. Bs's Writer opens `notes.md` in 9%. At the other sizes the macro agrees with the corrected value (4B 13/5, 8B 59/78, 32B 95/24).

So the sentence that the open rates "leave the stored reply's deficit at 14B unexplained" is false. The open rates explain it fully, and at 32B too (95% vs 24%). The Bs arm therefore changes more than the writing mode:

- the file name (a fixed `notes.md` vs model-chosen names such as `q1_facts.txt`);
- the number of files (one vs one per question);
- the location (root vs `docs/` at 14B);
- a ranking side effect: at 14B, `docs/Q*` sorts before `docs/d*` in `list_files`.

The Writer then ignores `notes.md` 76-95% of the time at 4B, 14B, and 32B. The +21.3 and +8.2 "Reply->tool, files" effects, the large "Copy->files, reply" effects (-34.2, -30.0, -20.4), and the "interaction" in Table 1 therefore measure discoverability of a system-named file, not reply vs tool writing.

Fixes:
- (text/analysis) Fix the metric: match against the paths the Researcher wrote, with the `workspace/` prefix stripped.
- (text/analysis) Report open rates for both arms at all sizes, and re-describe the within-files contrast as a naming/discoverability effect.
- (text/analysis) Drop "not through files" from the claim about the tool-call write.
- (needs new runs) Add one arm that holds discovery fixed. Options: give the Writer the notes path in the prompt in both file arms; store the reply under the file name(s) the B Researcher chose for the same task; or rename B's notes to `notes.md`.

The same `docs` filter is used for `REightReply*Open` (p14). The C index has a related bug: `refresh_index` lists any file under `docs/` as "source document". In the size sweep, C's Researcher writes notes under `docs/` in 21 of 200 tasks (14B) and 28 of 200 (32B), so the index mislabels those notes as source documents.

### 2. The recommendation "write results as plain replies" rests on a fragile write effect. (text/analysis only)

After Holm, the tool-call write is significant only at 4B. The other evidence is weaker than the text implies:

- **8B is entirely a 2Wiki effect.** HotpotQA gives 0.0 (-4.4 to 4.4) and 2Wiki gives -9.3 (-15.1 to -4.4).
- **Under exact match, the 8B effect's interval includes zero** (-3.3, -6.4 to 0.2), and so do both 8B delivery effects.
- **Part of the 4B effect is truncation and rescue.** In p16 at 4B, the A1w Researcher hit the 4,096-token cap in 14 of 150 tasks and needed the forced write in 18. Excluding those tasks shrinks the effect from -8.7 to -6.6 (-9.8 to -3.0). At 8B, 4 A1w tasks had empty notes.

The paper says the cap was raised "so that fewer tool-call writes of the notes are cut off" but never says how many still were. It already computes an "uncut" variant for p10 (`RSix*WriteUncut`) but does not report it.

Fixes:
- Report the per-dataset split, EM-based effects, and the excluding-truncation effect for Table 1's first row.
- State that p10 and p16 rerun the same tasks, so their agreement is a replication across reruns, not across task samples.
- Phrase the recommendation as "at 4B, and directionally at 8B and 32B".

### 3. File-channel accuracy largely measures the harness rescue, and this is reported only for the main run. (text/analysis only)

In the crossed runs, B's and Bs's Writers are rescued in **150 of 150** tasks at 4B; no 4B Writer ever writes `draft.md` voluntarily. Draft accuracy is 17-19%, and the Reviewer recovers it to about 35%. At 14B, Bs's Reviewer is rescued in 150 of 150 tasks against 105 for B.

At 4B, then, the "Copy->files" effects of -34 and -22 points mostly measure the 4B model's failure to use the tool protocol, not the cost of shared memory. In general, rescue rates differ across arms that the paper compares directly.

Fixes:
- Add a small table of rescue rates per arm, run, and size, with strict accuracy for Table 1.
- Say explicitly that the 4B file results are dominated by tool-protocol failure.

### 4. The serving-time claim is broader than its evidence. (text/analysis only, plus needs new runs)

Serving time was measured only:
- at k=3;
- with Qwen3-8B;
- on the PRO 6000 with a 100k cache (and 400k at 32-way concurrency);
- on stock vLLM.

The prefill results come from the RTX 3090 with a 35k cache on a modified vLLM (item 5). Yet the abstract says zero-copy "computes 38-49% of broadcast's prefill at up to five questions per task but saves no serving time".

Zero-copy also generates 1.2x broadcast's output tokens, so the time comparison mixes the ordering's effect on output length with the prefill savings. The numbers already computed tell a more precise story, and none of them appear in the text:
- zero-copy's server-side prefill time is **0.49x** broadcast's (`RSixPrefillTimeAZc`);
- its decode time per output token is lower (14.9 vs 16.9 ms);
- its summed request latency at 32-way concurrency is 0.87x (`REightHiReqAZc`).

Fixes:
- (text) Scope the claim to k=3, 8B, PRO 6000.
- (text) Report the prefill-time ratio and time per generated token.
- (needs new runs) Time the channels with outputs held fixed, for example by replaying recorded completions with a forced length. Measure on the 3090, where prefill is a larger share, and at k=5 and 8.

The serving runs are also single per channel per day (three runs total), executed sequentially on one GPU. The min-max spread is reported only in the macros, not in the text. Adding it is cheap.

### 5. Every RTX 3090 run used a locally modified vLLM, which the paper does not disclose. (text/analysis only; a small rerun would settle it)

`env.json` for t07_main, t08_*, and t09_* records `vllm_src_commit: 4ba787e, vllm_src_dirty: true`. That source tree (`/mnt/sda/heojin/cs506-store/vllm`) has uncommitted changes to `block_pool.py`, `kv_cache_manager.py`, `kv_cache_utils.py`, `single_type_kv_cache_manager.py`, and the GPU model runner, which are the files that determine prefix-cache hits and eviction. The PRO 6000 runs use stock vLLM.

The paper says "vLLM v0.33.0 with prefix caching" for all runs. Figure 2's and Figure 4's prefill numbers, and every 3090-vs-PRO comparison, therefore cross vLLM builds as well as GPUs.

There is some reassuring evidence. At k=8 with a 35k cache, zero-copy computes 0.83x broadcast's prefill on the 3090 and 0.85x on the PRO 6000 (p01_cache_35k).

Fixes: disclose the modification and argue it is inert when no labels are sent, or rerun 50 main-run tasks on stock vLLM and report the `cached_tokens` and accuracy agreement.

### 6. Runs that bear on the claims are omitted. (text/analysis only)

- **The D (handles) channel is omitted**, although it was run in the main run and in all four size runs. D is the design the introduction cites as production practice ("store subagent results and pass references"). It reaches 65.1% accuracy at 0.59x broadcast's prefill. The paper says "We measure five channels" and the figures show five.
- **The Br control (p14) is omitted.** In Br, the Writer and Reviewer read the workspace through tools but reply in plain text. Their accuracy falls 19.1 points (8B) and 31.1 points (32B) below B, and the Writer opens the notes in 30% (8B) and **0%** (32B) of tasks, against 59% and 95% in B. This directly shows that the file channels' accuracy depends heavily on output-instruction wording, which matters for generality and for item 1.
- **Bb (batched reads) and the 4-agent chain are also unreported.**

Fix: one sentence or a footnote for each, or a compact appendix table. Readers should know that the measured file-channel penalty is very sensitive to how the prompt is worded.

### 7. The statement that drift on the PRO 6000 is unmeasured is not true, because the data to measure it exists. (text/analysis only)

p10 and p16 run A1r, A1w, and B on the same 150 tasks at each of the four sizes. The rerun-minus-original difference is at most 0.9 points for every arm and size, with every interval within -3.6 to +3.1. That is a PRO 6000 repeat control at four model sizes.

Fix: report it, and replace "may include unmeasured drift" with the measured bound. This strengthens the cross-run claims (the 24-call budget, the 32B rerun, k=8 one vs three).

### 8. AlignScore fact coverage is used for mechanistic claims it cannot carry. (needs new runs, small: annotation only)

Kappa 0.29 against a Qwen3-8B judge on real notes is weak, and the judge comes from the same model family as the agents. The synthetic AUC (0.996) is measured on an easy task (sentence kept vs removed or altered). Even so, Figure 3a and the sentence "its notes state fewer supporting facts, 32% against 43% at 8B" carry the causal story for the write effect.

Tool-written notes are also much shorter (4B: 895 vs 1,776 characters; 32B: 992 vs 1,706). The coverage drop may therefore just be length.

Fix: human-label about 200 (fact, notes) pairs stratified by arm, or use a stronger judge from another family, and report notes length next to coverage.

### 9. Zero-copy changes more than the order, and its equivalence is stated on point estimates. (text/analysis only)

A1-zc moves the role instruction from the system prompt to a trailing user message (`build_messages`). This change in where the instruction sits could explain the 4B loss independently of KV reuse. The replay control rules out notes length, not instruction placement.

"Stays within 2.7 points of broadcast's in every setting" uses point estimates; the per-setting intervals with 150 tasks are about ±4 points. Figure 2(b)'s annotation "no significant loss" reads as equivalence. The pooled interval (-2.9 to 1.4) is the right statement.

Fixes: say that A1-zc also relocates the instruction; replace "no significant loss" with "loss at most 2.9 pts (95% CI)"; give per-setting intervals.

### 10. The scope of the multiple-comparison correction is uneven. (text/analysis only)

Holm covers only Table 1's 16 simple effects. Elsewhere the text counts uncorrected "significant in N of six settings" (A2: five of six; C over B: four of six). It also discusses the untested interaction ("whose interval lies above zero at all sizes but 8B") as evidence.

Given the number of runs and contrasts in the paper (well over 50 intervals), some will be spurious. The family for Holm is also chosen after the fact: p10's 12-test family gave two significant write effects, while p16's 16-test family gives one.

Fixes: pre-state the primary contrasts (for example: broadcast vs zero-copy, broadcast vs B, the four write effects, fan-out B) and Holm-correct those. Label everything else exploratory. Either test the interaction or stop leaning on it.

### 11. The repair results come from single runs on one model at one k, but the recommendations are stated generally. (needs new runs, or soften the text)

- Separate folders were tested only at k=8 (100 tasks, 8B).
- The write guard and heading rule were tested only at k=16 (60 and 59 tasks, 8B).
- The repairs were never compared at the same k.

Yet the abstract and conclusion say "parallel writers should get their own folders". Also, Bh has 59 tasks against B's 60, and the denominators of "recover 18.1 of its 30.4-point loss" differ.

Fixes: run Bf at k=16 and Bn/Bh at k=8 (about 100 tasks each, one GPU-night), or scope the recommendation to "Qwen3-8B, k=8".

### 12. A loose cross-run equivalence. (text/analysis only)

"Across runs, this loss of the file channels is as large as the stored reply's at 8B" compares the replay run with the crossed run. The two differ in GPU (3090 vs PRO 6000), vLLM build, token cap (2,048 vs 4,096), notes source (A1's notes vs A1r-style notes), and file header. The comparison is two point estimates with intervals of about ±3 points each. Given item 1, the stored reply is not a clean reference anyway.

Fix: delete the sentence or give the difference with an interval.

## Questions to the authors

1. Why does the Writer at 14B and 32B ignore a file named `notes.md` that `list_files` shows, when it is told to "find the notes with list_files"? Did you inspect the Writer's reasoning in Bs? Would telling the Writer the file name in both file arms remove the within-files effect?
2. What exactly do the uncommitted changes in the 3090 vLLM tree do? Is any of it active when requests carry no extra fields?
3. Why were D (handles) and Br omitted? D matches the production design cited in the introduction.
4. In A1w, `replies[r] = ws.written_by(r)` concatenates every file the Researcher wrote, in first-write order. How often did the A1w Researcher write more than one file or overwrite one, and does the effect differ in those tasks?
5. Is the "gold inside a prediction at most four words longer" rule symmetric across channels? Rescue-written answers might be longer. Please report Table 1 under EM (by my computation, 8B effects lose significance).
6. For the serving-time runs, were the channels run in a fixed order on the same GPU? Was anything else running on the host (CPU-side tokenization or tool execution) during those runs?
7. Is the 32B rerun (path normalization) paired against the first run's broadcast on the same tasks? Would you rerun broadcast with it to avoid the cross-run comparison?

## Sentences that overclaim or mismatch the code or numbers

1. "At 4B and 14B, the Writer seldom opens notes in either file arm (5% and 13% at 4B, 9% and 17% at 14B), so the open rates leave the stored reply's deficit at 14B unexplained."
   - Wrong for B at 14B. The Writer opens the Researcher's notes (saved as `docs/Q*_facts.txt`) in 99% of tasks. The `startswith("docs")` filter in `round10()` misclassifies them. The open rates do explain the 14B deficit (99% vs 9%).
2. Abstract: "but a plain reply stored as a file does no better than tool-written files"; Section 1: "but not through files, where a plain reply stored as a file does no better than tool-written notes."
   - At 14B and 32B the stored reply is 21.3 and 8.2 points worse. The cause is that the Writer does not open `notes.md`, not how the notes were written (weakness 1).
3. Section heading "The tool-call write costs accuracy on copied notes, not through files."
   - The "not through files" half rests on the Bs confound.
4. Abstract: "computes 38–49% of broadcast's prefill at up to five questions per task but saves no serving time."
   - Serving time was measured only at k=3, 8B, PRO 6000. Zero-copy's prefill time is 0.49x, and it generates 1.2x the output tokens.
5. "Less prefill does not mean less serving time, because decoding dominates."
   - Partly. Zero-copy also generates 20% more output. A fixed-output timing is needed to attribute the result to decode dominance.
6. "on vLLM v0.33.0 with prefix caching"
   - The RTX 3090 runs used a modified (dirty) v0.33.0 tree with changes to the KV-cache manager and block pool.
7. "We measure five channels on two multi-hop QA benchmarks and four model sizes"
   - Six channels were run in the main run and the size sweep (D omitted).
8. "It covers only the RTX 3090 servers, so cross-run comparisons on the RTX PRO 6000 may include unmeasured drift."
   - p10 vs p16 measures it: at most 0.9 points at all four sizes.
9. "Moving replies from copying to files changes accuracy by −34.2 to −5.1 points across the four sizes"
   - This is mostly the effect of the Writer not opening `notes.md` (5–24% at 4B, 14B, and 32B), and at 4B of 100% Writer rescue. It is not a property of file delivery as such.
10. Conclusion: "Agents should write results as plain replies and copy them forward. A tool-call write lowers accuracy when its notes are copied, at three of four sizes"
    - Uncorrected. After Holm, one of four. At 8B the effect is confined to 2Wiki and is not significant under EM.
11. Conclusion: "Parallel writers should get their own folders"
    - One model, one k (8), 100 tasks. Folders were not tested at k=16, where the guard and heading results come from.
12. "Across runs, this loss of the file channels is as large as the stored reply's at 8B."
    - Cross-GPU, cross-build, cross-cap comparison of two point estimates against a confounded reference.
13. Figure 2(b) annotation: "−61% prefill, no significant loss".
    - Non-significance is not equivalence. State the interval instead.
14. "Zero-copy's accuracy stays within 2.7 points of broadcast's in every setting"
    - Point estimates. The per-setting intervals are wider.
