# Style and grammar review, round 9

Paper: `paper/main.tex` (line numbers are source lines, approx.), checked against `main.pdf` via `pdftotext`.
Guide: `cs632-store/style_guide.md`, with most weight on "Clarity and grammar".

## Overall assessment

The prose is plain and mostly sounds like a careful systems researcher. Sentences are short, "we" is the
actor, banned words are gone, and most results come with a reason. What still marks it as machine-edited
is density, not word choice. Several paragraphs read as number ledgers: the decode-time paragraph (L204-217)
has about 20 numbers, the tool-call-write paragraph (L230-252) about 25, and the abstract 14. Every Results
and Sensitivity paragraph has the same shape: claim heading, setup, number with interval, second number
with interval, then a cross-run caveat. A tag phrase ("a cross-run comparison") and "unless stated" repeat.
The clarity problems the authors asked about are real and frequent. (1) Core terms are used before they
are defined, or under several names. "Channel" is never defined. "Setting", "main run", "plain files",
"fan-out loss", "heading rule", and "writing-control run" appear before any definition. Prefill is also
called "tokens computed", "compute", and "billed tokens" is used for "tokens sent". (2) Pronouns point to
the wrong noun, e.g. L215 "There it generates", where "it" follows "B" but means zero-copy. (3) The paper
uses an anthropomorphic verb in its headline observation ("prefill disagrees with"). (4) A unit error
repeats in four places ("answers N points fewer questions"). (5) Signed numbers are typeset as text
hyphens, and one breaks across a line ("(-36.4 to -" / "26.0)"). (6) Many introductory phrases with $k$,
a model size, or a condition lack a comma. Almost all fixes below keep or cut length. Items that add words
are A4, I7, I10, M3, M5, R11, S15, and K5. Cutting R25 and R37 and taking W7 and A6 more than pays for
them. If the page budget is tight, drop I7 and I10 first.

**Machine-likeness score: 4/10.** The voice is human. The ledgers, the uniform paragraph shape, and the
repeated tags are what remain.

Problem types: 1 = bare reference; 2 = comma/punctuation/typesetting; 3 = unclear antecedent or term used
before definition; 4 = anthropomorphic/figurative metric phrasing; 5 = grammar (articles, agreement,
parallelism, tense, hyphenation, incomplete comparison/missing baseline); 6 = AI-writing tell; 7 = must be
read twice.
